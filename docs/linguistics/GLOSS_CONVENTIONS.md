# ISL Gloss Conventions

## Status and governance

These conventions define the initial internal annotation approach. They are hypotheses for
native ISL review, not a complete grammar and not authorization to ship unreviewed output.
Examples illustrate structure and must be validated before entering the governed lexicon.

## Word order

Do not transliterate source-language word order. Preserve meaning and use the concept sequence
approved for the specific ISL construction and discourse context. Topic and context are
typically established before the comment, but no single order should be applied mechanically
to every sentence.

Each semantic unit is a separate token. Uppercase labels aid review; capitalization does not
turn a source-language word into an ISL sign.

## Concept ordering

Establish time, location, topic, participants, and event relationships in the order supported
by the reviewed construction. Omitted source-language function words must not become empty
tokens. Conversely, spatial, classifier, or discourse information required by ISL may need an
explicit governed token or future marker.

Pronouns and referents must remain consistent across a segment. A system must not create a
spatial locus unless the representation can preserve and validate it.

## Verb handling

Represent the event concept rather than English tense morphology. Time is established through
context or explicit time tokens. Agreement, directionality, aspect, and repetition may affect
meaning and require versioned lexical variants or future structured features; they must not be
invented from an English verb ending.

Auxiliary verbs are retained only when they express an independently signed concept in the
reviewed construction. Phrasal and idiomatic verbs are interpreted semantically rather than
split literally.

## Negation

Negation is an explicit governed concept and may also require non-manual marking. Its scope must
be represented unambiguously in the ordered gloss or a future scoped-marker model. Do not infer
that one generic `NOT` token is correct for every negative construction; lexical forms such as
`NOT-YET`, absence, prohibition, and inability require separate review.

## Questions

Question type must be preserved: yes/no, information-seeking, choice, rhetorical, or
confirmation. Wh-concepts are ordered according to the reviewed ISL construction rather than
source punctuation. Required eyebrow, gaze, head, or body markers are recorded only after the
non-manual annotation model is approved. A question mark alone is not a linguistic annotation.

## Classifiers

Classifiers encode referent class, handling, movement, location, size, or shape and cannot be
reduced safely to a generic lexical token. Use category `classifier` only when the construction,
referent, and reviewer-approved meaning are known. Until handshape, movement, orientation,
location, and spatial reference can be represented, unresolved classifier constructions must
remain unknown rather than guessed.

## Compound concepts

A concept expressed as one established compound uses one canonical hyphenated gloss label and
one stable token ID. Productive sequences remain separate ordered tokens. A source-language
multiword phrase is not automatically an ISL compound, and a source-language single word may
require multiple ISL concepts. Compound admission requires attested source evidence, native
review, regional classification, and versioned provenance.

## Annotation principles

- Preserve semantic scope and referent identity.
- Prefer approved concepts over literal source tokens.
- Mark uncertainty and unknowns explicitly.
- Never fabricate sign names, classifiers, or non-manual behavior.
- Keep gloss labels, linguistic tokens, sign assets, and animations as separate artifacts.
- Record regional variants without declaring one variant universally canonical.
